Tennis Biomechanics: Real Power with Zero Arm Strain

Many players in Peabody who play multiple times a week suffer from elbow pain or shoulder tightness due to improper stroke mechanics. Coach Mike’s instruction focuses on biomechanical efficiency:

  • The Kinetic Chain Forehand: Replacing arm "muscling" with lower-body loading, core rotation, and natural wrist lag that adds heavy topspin and ball penetration with less physical exertion.
  • High-Percentage First and Second Serves: Learning the continental grip, rhythmic toss placement, and upward shoulder drive to deliver reliable slice and kick serves that opponents cannot attack.
  • Defensive Footwork and Recovery: Utilizing split-steps, crossover steps, and open-stance sliding to recover quickly to the center of the court.

Mastering the Pickleball Transition in Peabody

Pickleball participation across Peabody is exploding, but players who rely solely on driving the ball often struggle against disciplined 3.5+ opponents. Michael's coaching focuses on:

  • Neutralizing Bangers: Learning how to absorb pace at the baseline or mid-court and drop the ball softly into the opponent's kitchen.
  • Patience at the NVZ (Kitchen Line): Developing smooth, unattackable cross-court dinks and reading body language before executing a speedup.
  • Court Positioning & Stacking: Mastering doubles coverage, communicating on middle balls, and poaching weak third shots.
