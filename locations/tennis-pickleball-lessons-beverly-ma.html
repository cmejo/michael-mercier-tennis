Tailored Tennis Coaching: From Biomechanics to Match Strategy

Tennis is an athletic chess match, but without sound biomechanics, even the most determined players hit plateaus or develop painful injuries like tennis elbow and shoulder impingement. In Beverly, Michael utilizes the same kinematic chain training methods he honed over two decades at Harvard:

  • Forehand Lag and Snap: Learn how to engage your hips, core, and non-dominant arm to produce effortless topspin and heavy ball speed without arming the ball.
  • Serve Architecture: Transition from a "waiter's tray" push to a fluid continental grip with true pronation, shoulder tilt, and explosive leg drive.
  • High-Speed Slow-Motion Video Analysis: View frame-by-frame recordings of your contact point, footwork recovery, and swing path during the lesson to see immediate visual proof of adjustments.

Beverly Pickleball: Leveling Up from Open Play to 4.5+ Mastery

Pickleball participation in Beverly has surged across city parks and local recreation centers. However, many players reach a barrier around the 3.0 to 3.5 skill rating because they rely exclusively on power drives and quick baseline exchanges.

Michael’s pickleball coaching zeroes in on the critical nuances that decide competitive games:

  • The Third Shot Drop: Perfecting the arc, soft grip tension, and push-motion necessary to neutralize aggressive baseline opponents and smoothly reach the kitchen line.
  • Kitchen Line Defense & Dinking: Developing patient, unattackable cross-court dinks, learning when to initiate speedups, and mastering fast-hands punch volleys at the Non-Volley Zone (NVZ).
  • Doubles Stacking and Court Geometry: Understanding rotational responsibilities, middle-court coverage, and poaching signals with your partner.
